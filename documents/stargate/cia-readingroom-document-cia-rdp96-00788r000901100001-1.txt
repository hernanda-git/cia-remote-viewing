SUMMARY ANALYSIS 


REMOTE VIEWING (RV) SESSION XXXII 


1. (S) This report provides documentation of a remote 
viewing session conducted for the purpose of orienting a 
remote viewer to the protocol which will be used in the 
remote viewing training program at Stanford Research 
Institute (SRI), Menlo Park, California. 


2. (S) The remote viewer's descriptions of the target 
provide very little objective correlation. On the other 
hand the session was very meaningful. The "beacon" was 
not at "a target" at the time of the session. An adminis- 
trative error in computing the proper on-target time for 
the session was made which resulted in the remote viewing 
of the "beacon" one hour before he knew he was to be on 
target. A subjective interpretation of the transcript 
shows this could have been why the remote viewer was 
concerned with whether or not the "beacon" knew he was 
under observation. The "beacon'' was in transit in a car 
at the time of the session. The remote viewer's image of 
a conquistador seems somehow symbolic of Panama, which 

is where the "beacon" was. 


3. (S) The protocol used for this session is detailed 
in the document, Grill Flame Protocol, AMSAA Applied 
Remote Viewing Protocol (S), undated. 


4. (S) Following is a transcript of the session. At TAB A 
are drawings made by the remote viewer reference his impres- 
sions of the target area. At TAB B is a post-session inter- 
view. No photographs were provided by the "beacon".

POST-SESSION INTERVIEW 


REMOTE VIEWING (RV) SESSION XXXII 


1. (S) Post-session interviews are conducted after the 
completion of a session to provide the selected remote 
viewer with the opportunity to express himself concerning 
his viewing experience. 


2. (S) #27.5 was satisfied with the results of this 
session even though there were some obvious problems. He 
felt his ability to detect the fact that #200 was not 
aware he was under observation was significant. After the 
session he stated that because he could not focus on one 
thing he thought the "beacon" might be moving in a vehicle. 
Several days later, when the "beacon' returned from Panama, 
this proved to be the case. 


3. (S) #27.5 and #66 enjoy working together. They seem 
to be able to communicate with one another based on similar 
cognitive structures. They both look forward to working 
together on remote viewing again.
